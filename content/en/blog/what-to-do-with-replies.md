## Speed first

A positive reply ==answered within the hour books a meeting far more often than the same reply answered the next day==. The prospect wrote while the problem was on their mind; a day later it is not. During business hours, someone must own the inbox and answer fast, in the sender's name and tone.

## Sort the replies

Every reply falls into one of five kinds, and each has a standard next step.

- **Positive.** Thank them, answer any question in one line, offer two time slots or a booking link.
- **Question.** Answer the question properly, then ask for the meeting.
- **Not now.** Ask when, note the date, write once on that date and not before.
- **Wrong person.** Ask for the right one; this is often the best reply of the campaign.
- **No.** Thank them, add them to the suppression list the same day, never write again.

Out-of-office replies are not replies; they are a reason to delay the follow-up by a week.

## Qualify in two questions

Before booking, ask the two things that tell you whether the meeting is worth both people's time: typically the size or situation that makes your offer fit, and the timing. Not a questionnaire; two lines inside a friendly reply. The answers go into the calendar invite so the person taking the meeting arrives prepared.

## Book without friction

A booking link beats proposing times by email, as long as it offers real availability in the prospect's time zone and the meeting is short. Fifteen to thirty minutes. Confirm the day before. If the prospect does not show, one polite rebooking message, then stop.

## Treat every reply as data

The questions prospects ask tell you what the email failed to explain. The objections tell you what the offer needs to address. The "wrong person" replies tell you how to target the next list. Review the replies every month and rewrite the sequence accordingly; this is where the campaign improves.

## The number to watch

Not reply rate: ==meetings booked per hundred positive replies==. If it is below sixty percent, the problem is almost always speed or friction, and both are fixable in a week.
